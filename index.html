Quantum Molecules

This page solves the quantum mechanics of a small molecule, live in your browser. Given where the nuclei are, it works out where the electrons go and what the molecule's energy is, using Hartree–Fock and density functional theory, the same methods chemists run on supercomputers for bigger molecules.

What the colors mean

  • The glowing cloud is the electron density, how likely you are to find an electron at each point. It is all the molecule's electrons together: a faint haze at the edges, bright and white near the nuclei where they crowd together.
  • The blue and orange surfaces are one molecular orbital, the shape of a single pair of electrons. Each orbital is a wave that is positive in some places and negative in others: blue is positive and orange is negative. Which one is which is arbitrary, but where they meet is a node, a surface where that orbital's electrons are never found. You start on the HOMO, the highest-energy orbital that holds electrons, which is where chemistry happens.
  • The balls are the atoms' nuclei in the usual chemistry colors (hydrogen white, carbon gray, nitrogen blue, oxygen red, fluorine green) and the sticks are bonds. The legend at the top names the ones on screen.

Things to try

  • Drag an atom. The calculation re-runs as you move it, so you can watch the energy rise and the cloud stretch.
  • Relax geometry lets the atoms slide downhill in energy to the molecule's natural shape.
  • Click a line in the orbital diagram on the right to draw that orbital. Lines with arrows hold electrons; empty lines are where an extra electron would go.
  • Play SCF replays the calculation from separate atoms to the finished molecule.
  • Drag empty space to rotate and scroll to zoom. Pick other molecules, methods and basis sets on the left.

Reading the numbers

Energies are in hartree (1 Ha = 27.2 eV, about 2600 kJ/mol). The HOMO–LUMO gap is roughly the energy it takes to excite the molecule, and the dipole moment, in debye, measures how unevenly the charge is spread.
