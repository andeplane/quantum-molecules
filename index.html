Quantum Molecules

You are looking at a real molecule, calculated from the laws of quantum mechanics right now, in your browser.

  • The glowing cloud is where the electrons are. Brighter means more electrons.
  • The balls are atoms, labelled with their element: H hydrogen, C carbon, N nitrogen, O oxygen.
  • Blue and orange shapes are orbitals, where one pair of electrons lives. You will meet them in a moment.

Tips at the bottom of the screen walk you through it: move an atom, let the molecule relax, look inside.

How does it work?

Given where the atoms' nuclei are, the program solves for the electrons: where they go and how much energy the molecule has. It uses Hartree–Fock and density functional theory, the same methods chemists run on supercomputers for bigger molecules, and repeats the whole calculation every time you move an atom.

The cloud is the electron density, all the electrons together. An orbital is the wave of one pair of electrons. Waves have a sign: blue is positive and orange is negative. Which is which is arbitrary, but where they meet is a node, where those electrons are never found. The orbitals that hold electrons add up to the cloud.

Energy levels on the right list the orbitals from most to least tightly bound. HOMO is the highest one holding electrons and LUMO the lowest empty one; chemistry mostly happens between the two. Energies are in hartree (1 Ha = 27.2 eV, about 2600 kJ/mol). Watch it solve replays the calculation as it improves its guess, step by step, from separate atoms to the finished molecule.

Calculation settings let chemists pick the method and basis set, set the charge and spin, or paste their own molecule.
